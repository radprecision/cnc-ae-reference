📐 Tolerance Matrix

CNC AE Reference Library — Module 5 · Five tolerance bands · Process · Tooling · Inspection · Cost

These tolerance bands represent production-capable values at Cpk ≥ 1.33Not theoretical best-case measurements — achievable in production with proper process control. Each tighter band requires a fundamentally different process, not just more care.
Band 1
±0.005″
Standard CNC — no special process required
General milling, turning, and drilling. Production-capable with standard tooling and fixturing. The default for non-critical features. No additional inspection beyond standard in-process gauging.
Band 2
±0.002″
Precision CNC — process discipline required
Precision milling, finish boring, reaming. Requires temperature stabilization, calibrated tooling, and verified work offsets. CMM recommended for first article. Achievable on well-maintained CNC equipment with process control.
Band 3
±0.001″
Near limit of CNC — tight process control
Approaching the practical limit of milling and turning without grinding. Requires thermal stabilization, in-process probing, and tight process control. CMM inspection on all critical features. Not suitable for general production without dedicated process development.
Band 4
±0.0005″
Grinding required — separate operation
Cylindrical or surface grinding required. Separate setup, separate operation, temperature-controlled environment recommended. CMM inspection required. Cost 2–4× band 2. ISO fit H6 or tighter.
Band 5
<±0.0002″
Specialty processes — honing, lapping, superfinishing
Honing for bores, lapping for flat surfaces, cylindrical grinding with in-process gauging. Temperature-controlled environment required. Specialist process. Cost 5–10× band 2. ISO fit H5 or tighter.

Band 3 — ±0.001″ (±0.025mm) — near the limit of CNC without grindingAchievable by fine boring, precision turning, or very controlled milling — but not reliably in production without dedicated process development. Cpk often marginal (1.00–1.33).
AttributeDetails
Tolerance±0.001″ / ±0.025mm
ProcessesFine boring (most reliable), precision turning with tool monitoring, precision milling with temperature stabilization and in-process probing

Cost vs Band 1+50–150%

Band 4 — ±0.0005″ (±0.013mm) — grinding requiredCNC turning and milling cannot reliably hold this tolerance in production. A separate grinding operation is required. This is a process step change, not just tighter machining.
AttributeDetails
Tolerance±0.0005″ / ±0.013mm
ProcessesCylindrical grinding (OD/ID), surface grinding (flat features), jig boring (location). Honing for bores in production.
Setup requirementsTemperature-controlled environment (±2°F) strongly recommended. Parts must be thermally stable before grinding. Grinding fluid temperature controlled.
ToolingGrinding wheel selection, dressing schedule, and spark-out passes are critical process parameters. Documented and controlled.
InspectionCMM required. Air gauging for bores in production. Roundness tester for cylindrical features. Measurement uncertainty must be <10% of tolerance.
Cpk capability≥ 1.33 achievable with controlled grinding process
Surface finishRa 0.1–0.4 µm (4–16 µin)
Cost vs Band 1+100–300% — separate operation, separate setup, separate inspection
ISO fitH5–H6 typical
Designing to Band 4 when Band 2 is sufficient is a common and expensive DFM errorAlways ask what the functional requirement is. A bearing bore that needs H7 (Band 2) specified as H6 (Band 4) doubles or triples the manufacturing cost for no functional benefit.

Measurement uncertainty rule — 10:1 gauge-to-tolerance ratioThe measurement system uncertainty must be less than 10% of the feature tolerance. A caliper with ±0.001″ uncertainty cannot reliably measure a ±0.001″ tolerance feature — the measurement uncertainty consumes the entire tolerance band. A Gauge R&R study is required before a measurement system is approved for production use on tight tolerances.

The most expensive DFM errors are Band 4 callouts on Band 2 featuresA ±0.0005″ tolerance on a bore that functionally requires H7 (±0.0008″) triggers a grinding operation for no benefit. This happens when designers default to tight tolerances without considering the manufacturing process. The AE's job is to catch this before the job starts.
