1. Tolerance tighter than process capability
HIGH SEVERITY
Symptom:
±0.001″ or tighter on features that will be milled or turned without grinding.
Fix:
Relax to ±0.002″ or add a grinding operation. Use ISO fit designations (H7/g6) which define function without over-specifying method.
Cost:
Tighter than ±0.001″ = +50–300% (grinding required).
Rule:
±0.005″ = standard CNC · ±0.002″ = precision · ±0.001″ = near limit · <±0.001″ = grind
2. Sharp internal corners
HIGH SEVERITY
Symptom:
R0 or no radius on pocket/slot corners. CAD models with true sharp internal corners.
Fix:
Add radius ≥ tool radius. Rule of thumb: R ≥ pocket depth / 3. Square corners require EDM — add 3–10× cost.
3. Thin walls
MEDIUM SEVERITY
Min wall:
Steel: 0.060″ · Aluminum: 0.040″ · Titanium: 0.080″
Effect:
Wall deflects under cutting forces, springs back — feature out of tolerance on CMM.
4. Over-specified surface finish
MEDIUM SEVERITY
Fix:
Apply Ra 3.2 µm as default. Specify tighter only on surfaces with documented functional reason.
Cost:
Ra 3.2→1.6: +15–25% · Ra 1.6→0.8: +30–50% · Ra 0.8→0.4: +100–200% (grinding)
5. Undercuts requiring special tooling
MEDIUM SEVERITY
Fix:
Redesign to eliminate, or specify lollipop/T-slot/back-chamfer cutter. Note and price the tooling.
Cost:
+40–100% for special tooling + additional setup.
6. Excess thread depth
LOW SEVERITY
Rule:
Steel: max 1.5× nominal diameter. Aluminum: max 2× nominal. Beyond this adds no pull-out strength.
Relief:
Cut tap needs 3–5 pitches relief at bottom. Form tap: 1–2 pitches. Thread mill: none needed.

Frame every finding as: Here is what I found → here is what it means for cost/risk/schedule → here is the option that solves it. Let the customer decide.
Tight tolerance finding
DON'T"We can't hold that tolerance." / "That's going to be very expensive."
DO"I flagged the ±0.001″ callout on the bore. With standard CNC turning, our Cpk runs about 1.1 at that tolerance — technically passing but little margin. Two options: add a grinding step (gives Cpk 1.6 consistently), or if the function is a sliding fit, an H7 tolerance class lets us ream to size which costs less than grinding. What's driving the tight dimension — is it a fit or a specific clearance?"
Sharp corner finding
DON'T"You have square corners — we can't machine those."
DO"The pocket corners are called out as square. For this depth, the smallest radius we can machine is about R0.050. If the mating part also has a square corner — is that functional or just how the model was built? If we genuinely need sharp corners, we can EDM them — that adds roughly 3–4 hours per corner. If R0.050 works, it's included in the standard milling price."
Written DFM findings
Written DFM findings outperform verbal ones every time. A numbered list with cost/risk and recommended fix — sent by email — creates a paper trail, gives the customer something to share with their engineer, and establishes due diligence. If a DFM issue was flagged in writing before the job started, the liability position is completely different than if it was mentioned verbally or not at all.
