🎨 Chip Color Reference

CNC AE Reference Library — Module 6 · SEPARATE tables for steel vs stainless — colors mean different things

⚠ Critical distinction — Steel vs Stainless Blue chips in steel = acceptable in roughing. Blue chips in stainless = FAILURE IMMINENT. Always identify the material before interpreting chip color. The same color means opposite things depending on the workpiece material.
Carbon and alloy steel (1018, 4140, 4340, A36). Chip color is a reliable heat indicator. Blue is acceptable in heavy roughing but should not appear in finishing operations.
ColorTemperature (est.)AssessmentMeaning & Action
Silver / StrawBelow 400°FGOODLow heat at cutting zone. Ideal for finishing. Good chip load and SFM combination. No action needed.
Gold / Yellow400–500°FGOODNormal roughing temperature. Heat is controlled. Acceptable for general roughing. Monitor for progression to purple/blue.
Purple / Violet500–600°FMONITORElevated heat. Acceptable in heavy roughing but approaching limit. Check coolant flow, consider reducing SFM 10–15%.
Blue600–700°FHIGH HEATHigh heat at cutting zone. Acceptable ONLY in heavy roughing with flood coolant. Not acceptable in finishing. Reduce SFM or increase coolant if consistent. Edge wear accelerates above this point.
Dark Blue / BlackAbove 750°FREDUCEExcessive heat. Insert life shortening rapidly. Reduce SFM immediately — 15–25% reduction. Check for built-up edge. Verify coolant pressure and coverage.
⚠ STAINLESS STEEL — chip color interpretation is REVERSED from carbon steel. Blue chips in stainless indicate work hardening and imminent cutting edge failure. Do NOT continue cutting.
ColorAssessmentMeaning & Action
Silver (no color)TARGETOptimal. Low heat, no work hardening, good chip load. This is the target chip color for all stainless operations including roughing.
Light GoldMARGINALBorderline acceptable. Heat is building. Check chip load — may be too low (rubbing) or SFM too high. Verify coolant coverage on the cutting zone.
Purple / VioletWARNINGWork hardening is occurring. The HAZ (heat-affected zone) is work-hardening ahead of the tool. Reduce SFM 20%. Increase chip load to ensure the tool cuts below the hardened layer.
BlueSTOP — FAILURE IMMINENTWork hardening has progressed significantly. Cutting edge failure likely within the next few passes. STOP and inspect the insert immediately. The next pass may break the insert or produce a poor surface. Check: SFM too high? Chip load too low? Coolant adequate?
Dark Blue / BlackCRITICAL FAILUREInsert has likely already failed or is moments from failing. Stop immediately. Inspect cutting edge, inspect workpiece surface. Do not continue without replacing the insert.
Key rule for stainless: the chip load must be high enough to cut below the work-hardened layer from the previous pass. If chip load is too low, the tool rubs the hardened surface, generates more heat, hardens the next layer — and the condition spirals. Always err toward higher chip load in stainless.
Aluminum (6061, 7075, 2024). Color change is less pronounced than in steel — aluminum's lower melting point means failure modes differ. Watch for BUE (built-up edge) more than chip color.
Color / AppearanceAssessmentMeaning & Action
Bright silver, curlyOPTIMALCorrect chip load and SFM. Good chip evacuation. Long continuous chips are normal in aluminum finishing.
Dull silver, shortMONITORPossible BUE forming. Check cutting edge — aluminum welding to tool face. TiAlN or AlTiN coatings accelerate BUE — use uncoated carbide or ZrN coating for aluminum.
Gold tingeHIGH HEATUnusually high heat for aluminum. Check SFM (may be too low, causing rubbing) or verify correct alloy. 7075 runs hotter than 6061. Increase coolant or air blast.
Dark / smearedBUE / WELDINGAluminum is welding to the tool (BUE). Smeared finish on part. Replace or resharpen insert. Check: uncoated carbide? Correct geometry (sharp edge, high rake)? Coolant reaching the cut?
⚠ TiAlN and AlTiN coatings MUST NOT be used in aluminum — chemical affinity between aluminum in the workpiece and aluminum in the coating causes welding (BUE). Use uncoated carbide, ZrN, or TiN coating for aluminum.
Cast iron (gray GG, nodular GGG). Produces powder/dust chips, not curled chips. Chip color interpretation is different — watch for dust color and surface finish degradation. Preferred: DRY machining.
Chip appearanceAssessmentMeaning & Action
Fine gray/black powderNORMALCharacteristic chip form for gray cast iron. Dry preferred — coolant + graphite dust = abrasive slurry that accelerates guide and spindle wear.
Coarser flake chipsNORMAL — NODULARNodular (ductile) iron produces more conventional chips than gray iron. Less powdery, more chip-like. Coolant acceptable for nodular iron.
Glowing/sparking chipsHIGH HEATExcessive cutting speed or interrupted cut generating concentrated heat. Reduce SFM. Verify insert grade — cast iron grades (K-group) required.
Long stringy chipsUNEXPECTEDMay indicate wrong alloy (malleable iron or steel contamination) or incorrect insert geometry. Malleable iron machines more like steel — requires different insert selection.
Titanium (Ti-6Al-4V, commercially pure). Extremely heat-sensitive. Work hardening and built-up edge both occur. Chip color monitoring is critical — high heat damages the part metallurgy, not just the tool.
ColorAssessmentMeaning & Action
Silver / light strawTARGETOptimal for titanium. Low heat, no work hardening. Maintain these parameters. Flood coolant should be running at full pressure.
Gold / amberMARGINALHeat building. Acceptable in roughing if flood coolant is adequate. Not acceptable in finishing — surface integrity at risk. Reduce SFM 10%.
Purple / blueREDUCE IMMEDIATELYSignificant heat. Work hardening occurring. Metallurgical damage to part surface possible. Reduce SFM 20–30%. Verify TSC pressure (1000+ psi preferred for titanium drilling).
Dark / blackSTOPTitanium fire risk above 600°C in fine chips/dust. Surface integrity destroyed. Stop and inspect. Never allow dry machining of titanium — always flood or TSC.
⚠ Titanium chips can ignite above 600°C. Never allow titanium dust to accumulate — always use coolant. Ceramic tooling used dry for roughing Inconel should NEVER be used for titanium.
