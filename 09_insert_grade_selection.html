ISO P — Steel (blue color code)Largest material group. P1–P14 in ISCAR classification. Includes non-alloy, low-alloy, high-alloy, tool steel, and stainless ferritic/martensitic. Select grade by hardness and operation type.
OperationStarting grade rangeGrade characteristicsNotes
Heavy roughing / interruptedP40–P50 (tough)High cobalt substrate, thick PVD TiAlN or multilayerPrioritize toughness over wear resistance. Hardest grade that does not chip.
General roughingP25–P35Medium cobalt, CVD or PVD TiAlNMost common starting point for alloy steel roughing
Semi-finishingP15–P25Lower cobalt, thinner coatingBalance of toughness and wear resistance
FinishingP05–P15Fine grain, hard substrate, thin PVDMaximum hardness. Continuous cuts only.
Application range numbersLow number (P05) = harder/more wear-resistant. High number (P50) = tougher/more impact-resistant. Milling always starts higher than turning because milling is inherently interrupted.
ISO M — Stainless steel (yellow color code)Austenitic (304, 316), ferritic, martensitic, duplex. Work hardening is the critical challenge. PVD coatings preferred over CVD.
OperationGrade rangeKey requirement
Roughing austenitic (304, 316)M25–M35PVD TiAlN preferred. High positive rake — reduce cutting forces. Adequate chip load to cut below work-hardened layer.
Finishing austeniticM10–M20Sharp edge, high positive rake. Low chip load risks rubbing and work hardening.
Duplex stainlessM20–M35Higher toughness required — duplex has higher strength. PVD coating essential.
CVD coatings in stainless — proceed with cautionCVD coatings have tensile residual stress at the surface. In stainless where the cut is often borderline (low chip load, work hardening), this tensile stress combined with the aggressive stainless environment can accelerate edge chipping. PVD coatings with compressive residual stress are preferred for stainless steel.
ISO K — Cast iron (red color code)Gray GG, nodular GGG, malleable, compacted graphite. Produces powder/dust chips. Dry preferred for gray iron — coolant creates abrasive slurry. CBN for hardened cast iron (>45 HRC).
MaterialGrade rangeSpecial considerations
Gray cast iron (GG)K10–K20Dry machining preferred — no coolant. CVD Al₂O₃ coating ideal (ceramic-like hardness). Abrasion is primary wear mode.
Nodular/ductile iron (GGG)K15–K30More like steel — coolant acceptable. Higher toughness required than for gray iron.
Hardened cast iron (>45 HRC)CBN / PCBNCeramic or CBN required. Standard carbide unsuitable.
ISO N — Non-ferrous (green color code)Aluminum alloys, copper alloys, non-metallic. Uncoated carbide preferred for aluminum — TiAlN/AlTiN must NOT be used (chemical affinity → BUE). PCD for high-volume aluminum.
MaterialGradeCoatingCritical rule
Aluminum (6061, 7075)N05–N20, sharp edge, high rakeUncoated, ZrN, or TiN onlyNEVER TiAlN or AlTiN — chemical affinity causes BUE
High-silicon aluminum (>12% Si)N05–N15 or PCDUncoated or PCDSilicon is abrasive — short carbide life. PCD is the production solution.
Copper alloysN10–N20Uncoated or ZrNSharp edge essential. Some copper alloys work-harden.
ISO S — Super alloys (brown/orange color code)Inconel, Hastelloy, titanium alloys, cobalt alloys. Highest cutting forces, lowest thermal conductivity, maximum work hardening. Toughest material group to machine.
MaterialGrade rangeSpecial approach
Inconel 718 / HastelloyS15–S30, tough gradesPVD TiAlN or AlTiN. High pressure coolant essential. Ceramic for dry roughing — no interruptions, rigid setup, specific ceramic grades only.
Titanium Ti-6Al-4VS10–S25PVD TiAlN. Flood coolant always — fire risk above 600°C. Never ceramic (dry). High positive rake.
Cobalt alloys (Stellite)S25–S40Most difficult to machine. Very low SFM. Rigid setup mandatory.
Ceramic in Inconel: dry only, no interruptionsCeramic inserts for Inconel roughing must run dry at high SFM — coolant causes thermal shock and shatters the insert. Zero interruptions — interrupted cuts will break ceramic. Rigid setup is non-negotiable. Ceramic gives 5–10× higher MRR than carbide in Inconel roughing when conditions are right.
ISO H — Hardened materials (gray color code)Hardened steel 45–65 HRC, hardened cast iron, chilled cast iron. CBN (cubic boron nitride) is the primary tool material. Hard turning can replace cylindrical grinding when conditions are met.
HardnessTool materialConditions for hard turning
45–55 HRCCBN mixed grade or ceramicContinuous cuts preferred. No vibration. Rigid setup. Dry — never flood coolant on CBN (thermal shock).
55–65 HRCCBN high-CBN content gradeMaximum rigidity. Shallow DOC (0.010–0.030″). SFM 400–650.
>65 HRCPCD or grinding onlyCBN performance degrades above 65 HRC. Grinding or EDM typically required.
CBN — NEVER flood coolantFlood coolant on CBN inserts causes thermal shock that fractures the insert. Dry machining only for CBN. Air blast is acceptable if chips need clearing.
Toughness vs Hardness spectrum — carbide onlyThis spectrum applies to carbide inserts. CBN, ceramic, PCD, and cermet have narrow, fixed performance windows — they do not have a wide toughness-to-hardness spectrum.

Substrate levers (independent)

  • Grain size: Finer grain = harder, more wear resistant
  • Cobalt %: Higher cobalt = tougher, more impact resistant
  • These are independent — fine grain + high cobalt = hard AND tough (within limits)

Coating levers (independent)

  • CVD: Tensile stress, thicker, for high heat continuous cuts
  • PVD: Compressive stress, thinner, for interrupted cuts, stainless, titanium
  • Substrate and coating are independent — combine freely
Selection rule: hardest grade that does not chipStart with a harder grade. If chipping occurs, move to a tougher grade. Don't start tough and "hope" — tougher grades wear faster. Find the edge of chipping, then step back one grade.

CVD vs PVD summaryCVD = thicker, tensile stress, applied at high temperature. Best for high-heat continuous cuts in steel and cast iron. PVD = thinner, compressive stress, applied at lower temperature. Best for interrupted cuts, stainless, titanium, and any application where edge sharpness is critical.
