Key properties

  • Density: 0.098 lb/in³ (2.7 g/cm³) — 1/3 of steel
  • Melting point: ~1220°F (660°C)
  • Thermal conductivity: high — dissipates heat well
  • 6061-T6: 40 ksi tensile, 35 ksi yield, 95 HRB
  • 7075-T6: 83 ksi tensile, 73 ksi yield, harder to machine

Tooling recommendations

  • Uncoated carbide — preferred for most aluminum
  • ZrN or TiN coating — acceptable
  • NEVER TiAlN or AlTiN — chemical affinity → BUE
  • High positive rake geometry essential
  • 2 or 3 flute end mills for better chip evacuation
  • PCD for high-volume production or high-silicon alloys

TiAlN and AlTiN coatings are prohibited for aluminumThe aluminum content in the coating bonds chemically with the workpiece aluminum at cutting temperatures, causing built-up edge (BUE). The BUE tears out and leaves a rough, smeared surface finish. Use uncoated carbide or ZrN.

Key properties by grade

  • 1018 (mild): 64 ksi tensile, soft, free-machining
  • 4140 annealed: 95 ksi tensile, 197 HB
  • 4140 Q&T 28-32 HRC: 130–145 ksi tensile
  • 4340 Q&T: 150–180 ksi tensile
  • Hardness drives insert grade selection more than alloy designation

Tooling recommendations

  • CVD TiCN + Al₂O₃ for continuous turning
  • PVD TiAlN for milling and interrupted cuts
  • Harder grades (lower application number) for soft steel finishing
  • Tougher grades (higher application number) for hardened/interrupted
  • Above 40 HRC: approach H-group tooling strategies

Key properties

  • 304: 73 ksi tensile, 31 ksi yield, 200 HB — most common
  • 316: similar to 304 + Mo for corrosion resistance
  • Work hardening rate: 3–4× higher than carbon steel
  • Low thermal conductivity — heat stays at cutting zone
  • Galling tendency on metal-to-metal contact

Tooling recommendations

  • PVD TiAlN — preferred over CVD for stainless
  • High positive rake geometry essential
  • Sharp edge — worn edges work-harden the surface
  • Adequate chip load — NEVER let the tool rub
  • Flood coolant — high pressure preferred
  • Anti-galling compound on locating contacts in workholding

Key properties — Ti-6Al-4V

  • 138 ksi tensile, 128 ksi yield, 36 HRC equivalent
  • Density: 0.160 lb/in³ — 56% of steel
  • Thermal conductivity: very low — 1/6 of steel
  • Young's modulus: 16.5 Msi — 55% of steel (more springback)
  • Ignites above 600°C in fine chip/dust form

Tooling and setup requirements

  • PVD TiAlN coating — no uncoated carbide for volume work
  • High positive rake to minimize cutting forces
  • Flood coolant always — TSC 1000+ psi for drilling
  • Never ceramic — fire risk
  • Non-ferrous contact pads in fixtures — galling risk
  • Rigid setup — springback and deflection significant

Inconel 718 key properties

  • 185 ksi tensile, 150 ksi yield (aged condition)
  • Maintains strength to 1200°F — cutting heat stays in tool
  • Work hardening rate: extremely high
  • Thermal conductivity: very low — 8.3 BTU/hr·ft·°F
  • Specific cutting force Kc: ~110,000 psi (vs 70,000 for alloy steel)

Non-negotiable requirements

  • Maximum machine and fixturing rigidity — no compliance anywhere
  • PVD TiAlN or AlTiN — heat-resistant coating essential
  • High pressure TSC coolant (1000+ psi) for all drilling
  • Ceramic only for dry roughing — rigid, no interruptions, specific grades
  • Replace inserts at scheduled intervals — don't run to failure
  • No vibration — any chatter destroys insert life instantly

Gray cast iron (GG) properties

  • Compressive strength high, tensile strength low
  • Graphite flakes cause brittle fracture → powder chips
  • Good vibration damping — used for machine bases and fixtures
  • Machinability rating: ~60–70% (easier than steel)
  • Free-machining but abrasive — graphite is a solid lubricant but the dust is abrasive to machine ways

Tooling and coolant

  • CVD Al₂O₃ coating — chemically inert, ideal for gray iron
  • TiC coating also effective for abrasion resistance
  • Dry machining preferred for gray iron — coolant + graphite dust = abrasive slurry
  • Nodular (ductile) iron: coolant acceptable, machines more like steel
  • Carbide grades K10–K20 for gray, K20–K30 for nodular

Common engineering plastics

  • Delrin (acetal): easiest to machine, low friction
  • Nylon: good strength but absorbs moisture — check final dims after conditioning
  • PEEK: high performance, expensive, machines well when sharp
  • Ultem (PEI): excellent stability, less creep than nylon
  • UHMW-PE: very soft, gummy, sticks to tooling

Key rules for plastics

  • Sharp edge essential — dull tools generate heat and melt the material
  • High SFM, low chip load — opposite of metal instinct
  • Air blast or mist — flood coolant can cause swelling in some plastics
  • Measure after unclamping — plastics spring back significantly
  • Static charge attracts chips — air blast keeps surface clean
  • Vacuum workholding preferred for flat parts
