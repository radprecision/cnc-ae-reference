CNC AE Reference Library — Module 4 · ASME Y14.5 · 14 symbols · FCF anatomy · Datum structure · Modifiers

Five categories — Form · Profile · Orientation · Location · RunoutForm tolerances never reference a datum. Profile can reference datums or not. Orientation, location, and runout always reference at least one datum.
Form — no datum reference
⏥
Flatness FORM
Surface · No datum
All points on the surface must lie between two parallel planes separated by the tolerance value. Does not control location or orientation — only the shape of the surface itself.
○
Circularity (Roundness) FORM
Feature of size · No datum
At any cross-section, all points on the surface must lie between two concentric circles separated by the tolerance value. Applied per cross-section — does not control the full cylinder.
⌭
Cylindricity FORM
Feature of size · No datum
All points on the cylindrical surface must lie between two coaxial cylinders separated by the tolerance value. Controls roundness, straightness, and taper simultaneously. Tight and expensive to inspect.
⏤
Straightness FORM
Line or axis · No datum
Each line element on a surface must lie within two parallel lines separated by the tolerance. When applied to an axis (with diameter symbol ⌀) — the derived axis must lie within a cylindrical tolerance zone.
Profile — may or may not reference datums
⌒
Profile of a Line PROFILE
Line element · Datum optional
Each line cross-section of the surface must lie within a tolerance zone defined by two offset profiles. Used for 2D contours — arcs, transitions, non-standard profiles.
⌓
Profile of a Surface PROFILE
Full surface · Datum optional
The entire surface must lie within a tolerance zone defined by two offset surfaces. The most powerful GD&T control — can replace all other tolerances on complex 3D surfaces. Common in aerospace and medical.
Orientation — always references a datum
∠
Angularity ORIENT
Surface or axis · Datum required
The surface or axis must lie within two parallel planes (or a cylinder) at the specified angle relative to the datum. The angle is basic — exact, with no tolerance on the angle itself.
⊥
Perpendicularity ORIENT
Surface or axis · Datum required
Special case of angularity at 90°. The surface or axis must lie within the tolerance zone perpendicular to the referenced datum. Most common orientation control for bores, bosses, and mating faces.
∥
Parallelism ORIENT
Surface or axis · Datum required
Special case of angularity at 0°. The surface or axis must lie within two planes parallel to the referenced datum. Controls orientation only — does not control distance from the datum (that requires a size or location tolerance).
Location — always references datums
⊕
True Position LOCATION
Feature of size · Datum required
The axis or center plane of the feature must lie within a tolerance zone (cylindrical or between two planes) located at the true (basic) position. The most widely used GD&T control. Diameter symbol = cylindrical zone. Bonus tolerance available at MMC.
◎
Concentricity LOCATION
Axis · Datum required
The derived median points of the feature must lie within a cylindrical tolerance zone coaxial with the datum axis. Extremely difficult to inspect — requires CMM. Usually replaced by runout or position in modern practice.
⊙
Symmetry LOCATION
Median plane · Datum required
The derived median points must lie within two parallel planes symmetric about the datum. Like concentricity but for planar features. Also difficult to inspect — often replaced by position in modern practice.
Runout — always references a datum axis
↗
Circular Runout RUNOUT
Per cross-section · Datum axis required
As the part is rotated about the datum axis, the indicator reading at any single cross-section must not exceed the tolerance. Controls circularity + coaxiality at each cross-section independently. Simpler to inspect than total runout.
⇗
Total Runout RUNOUT
Full surface · Datum axis required
As the part is rotated and the indicator traverses the full surface, the total reading must not exceed the tolerance. Controls circularity, cylindricity, coaxiality, and taper simultaneously. Most comprehensive runout control.

Reading the example FCF

⊕ ⌀0.010 Ⓜ | A | B | C

  • True position (⊕)
  • Cylindrical tolerance zone ⌀0.010
  • At maximum material condition (Ⓜ) — bonus tolerance available
  • Referenced to datum A (primary), B (secondary), C (tertiary)

⌀ in the tolerance — what it means

Without ⌀: tolerance zone is two parallel planes (width = tolerance value). Used for slots, tabs, planar features.

With ⌀: tolerance zone is a cylinder (diameter = tolerance value). Used for holes, pins, bosses. A ⌀0.010 cylindrical zone gives ~41% more area than a 0.010 square zone — this is the main benefit of true position over coordinate tolerancing.

Datums are theoretical perfect planes, axes, and points — not the physical surfacesThe physical surface is the datum feature. The datum is derived from it. A flat surface establishes a datum plane. A cylindrical bore establishes a datum axis. The datum reference frame (DRF) is the coordinate system built from the three datums.

Datum reference frame — 3-2-1

  • Primary datum (A): contacts 3 points minimum. Establishes the base plane. Removes 3 DOF (Z translation + rotation about X and Y).
  • Secondary datum (B): contacts 2 points minimum. Removes 2 DOF (Y translation + rotation about Z).
  • Tertiary datum (C): contacts 1 point minimum. Removes 1 DOF (X translation).
  • Total: 6 DOF constrained — part fully located.

Datum feature selection rules

  • Primary datum = largest, most stable surface — maximizes contact
  • Datums must be machined features — not raw stock or saw cuts
  • Datums should be accessible for fixturing and inspection
  • Datum precedence (A before B before C) must match the fixturing sequence
  • The datum that controls the most critical relationship is primary

Datum order matters — A|B|C is not the same as B|A|CThe order of datum references in the FCF defines the precedence of constraint. Changing datum order changes what the tolerance zone is relative to. A drawing that specifies A|B|C locates the feature relative to A first — a different result than B|A|C even with the same physical datums.
Material condition modifiers only apply to features of sizeA feature of size has opposing surfaces — holes, pins, slots, tabs. Flat surfaces, edges, and line elements cannot have material condition modifiers. Applying Ⓜ to a surface flatness callout is an error.
Ⓜ
Maximum Material Condition (MMC)
The condition where the feature contains the most material. For a hole: smallest allowable diameter. For a pin: largest allowable diameter.

Bonus tolerance: as the feature departs from MMC toward LMC, the geometric tolerance increases by the same amount. A ⌀0.010 position at MMC on a hole that is 0.005 larger than MMC = ⌀0.015 actual tolerance zone.

Use when: assembly fit is the function. The loosest fit (smallest hole + largest pin) defines the worst case.
Ⓛ
Least Material Condition (LMC)
The condition where the feature contains the least material. For a hole: largest allowable diameter. For a pin: smallest allowable diameter.

Bonus tolerance: same principle as MMC but in the opposite direction — as the feature departs from LMC toward MMC, the geometric tolerance increases.

Use when: minimum wall thickness is the function. Ensures adequate material remains around a hole or between features. Less common than MMC.
Ⓢ
Regardless of Feature Size (RFS)
The geometric tolerance applies at any size within the size tolerance — no bonus tolerance. The tolerance zone is always the stated value regardless of the actual feature size.

Implied: RFS is the default in ASME Y14.5-2009 and later. No symbol is needed — absence of Ⓜ or Ⓛ means RFS.

Use when: the function requires the tolerance to be maintained regardless of size — balance, dynamic fit, precision location.

CMM vs functional gaugeCMM inspection is the standard and most flexible method — measures actual coordinates and calculates the geometric deviation mathematically. Functional gauges (hard gauges) are faster for production and directly simulate assembly, but only work at MMC and give go/no-go results without measurement data. For PPAP and capability studies, CMM data is required.

Basic dimensions are exact — they have no toleranceAny dimension associated with a GD&T control is a basic dimension (shown in a box on the drawing). Basic dimensions have no tolerance of their own — all the tolerance is in the feature control frame. Treating a basic dimension as a ±0.005 tolerance is a common and serious error.
